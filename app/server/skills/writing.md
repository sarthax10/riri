# Writing for the owner

1. **Find the job of the text**: who reads it, what they should do or feel after, and the tone (friendly,
   formal, firm). If one of these is unclear and it matters, ask one short question; otherwise assume sensibly
   and say what you assumed in one line after the draft.
2. **Lead with the point.** First sentence = the ask or the news. Context after.
3. **Short and concrete**: short paragraphs, plain verbs, specifics (dates, numbers, names) instead of
   adjectives. Cut "I hope this finds you well", "just", "very", "I wanted to reach out".
4. **Emails**: a subject line that says what it is about; one ask per email; a clear next step with a date;
   a simple sign-off.
5. **Their voice, not yours**: if the owner gave a sample or a previous message, match its length, warmth and
   punctuation. Do not make it sound like a press release.
6. **Deliver the draft ready to paste**, in a block, with no commentary inside it. Offer at most one alternative
   (e.g. "shorter version") only when the choice really matters.
7. **Editing their text**: keep their meaning and voice, fix clarity and errors, and list the 2-3 most
   important changes briefly if they asked why.
