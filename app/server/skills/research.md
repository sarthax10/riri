# Researching on the web

1. **One good query beats three vague ones.** Use the key nouns plus a qualifier: a year, a version, "docs",
   "release notes", "review", "price India", a site name. Quote exact phrases or error messages.
2. **Pick, don't skim everything.** From the results choose the 1-2 most authoritative pages (official docs,
   the vendor, a primary source, a reputable outlet) and `read_webpage` only those. Snippets alone are fine for
   simple facts like a date or a score.
3. **Check freshness.** Note the date on what you read. For "latest", "current" or "today" questions prefer
   pages from the last few months; say when information may be out of date.
4. **Cross-check** anything surprising, numerical or important with a second independent source.
5. **Answer, then cite.** Lead with the answer in a sentence, add 2-4 key details, then sources as Markdown
   links: [Title](url). Never invent a URL or a quote.
6. **Stop early.** If two searches do not find it, say what you found and what is uncertain instead of
   searching on and on.

For prices and products: give the price with currency, the seller, and the date seen. For people and companies:
stick to what sources say; do not speculate about private individuals.
